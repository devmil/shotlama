03 / Features

What it does.

The first release covers still captures from shortcut to shared file. Screen recording follows after it.

Area, window and display

The screen holds still while you select. A loupe, edge snapping and exact sizes help you get the pixels you mean. Window captures can sit on your wallpaper.

Quick Access

Every capture waits in a card. Copy, save, drag into any app, pin or annotate with one gesture. The card never takes the focus away from what you are doing.

Annotate

Tapered arrows, shapes, text in three faces, counters, a highlighter and spotlight. Undo and redo go back as far as you need.

Backgrounds

Place a capture on a gradient, a colour, your wallpaper or colours taken from the capture itself, with padding, corners and a shadow.

Pin

Keep a capture floating above your work while you type or compare. Zoom it, fade it, or let clicks pass through to the window below.

Capture Text

Select an area and copy the text in it. Recognition runs on your computer with the engine your system provides.

History

Earlier captures stay at hand. Reopen one and every arrow and label is still editable. You choose how long History keeps them.

Keyboard all the way

Go from shortcut to clipboard without touching the pointer. Every shortcut can be changed, and conflicts are shown before they bite.

Recording Later

Video and GIF with audio, clicks and keystrokes, a trim editor, and later scrolling capture. Planned after the first release.

04 / Privacy

Your captures stay on your computer.

Screens show private things. ShotLama is built so that what you capture goes only where you send it.

  • No account and no cloud. Captures and History live on your disk.
  • Concealment is flattened into every copy and file. A blurred address cannot be restored from the output.
  • Saved images carry no metadata: no location, no device name, no timestamps.
  • The only network access is an update check, and only when you allow it.

05 / Platforms

One tool on three desktops.

Each system works a little differently. Where a desktop has its own picker or permission, ShotLama uses it and explains it once.

macOS

macOS 14 or later, on Apple silicon and Intel. The first platform to be finished and released.

Windows

Windows 10 version 2004 or later and Windows 11, 64-bit. Follows after macOS.

Linux

x86_64 on X11, KDE Plasma 6, GNOME 46 or later and wlroots compositors. On Wayland, the desktop’s screen-sharing portal takes part in each capture.
